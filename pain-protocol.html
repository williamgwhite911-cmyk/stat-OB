1.0 Analgesia

Assess pain frequently during transport to gauge severity and monitor the response to interventions. Pain assessment should include the use of scales appropriate for the age group and patient condition. Pain should be treated to a level as low as reasonably possible in balance with the patient's cardiovascular stability and pain goals.

Fentanyl stock 50 mcg/mL · 1st-line opioid
Adult
  • 0.5–1 mcg/kg IV, IM, IN
  • Repeat every 5–10 minutes PRN
  • Max cumulative dose 200 mcg
Pediatric
  • 1–2 mcg/kg IV / IN
  • Repeat every 5–10 minutes PRN
  • Max single dose 100 mcg
Hydromorphone (Dilaudid) stock 2 mg/mL or 4 mg/mL · longer-acting opioid
Adult
  • 0.2–0.8 mg IV over 2–3 min (most adults)
  • 0.5–1.5 mg IV over 2–3 min (opioid-tolerant)
  • Repeat q 30–60 min PRN, titrated to effect
Pediatric
  • 0.01–0.02 mg/kg IV over 2–3 min
  • Typical range 0.05–0.2 mg per dose
  • Repeat q 30–60 min PRN, titrated to effect
Adjunctive Analgesia
Ketamine — Sub-dissociative Dose stock 500 mg / 10 mL (50 mg/mL) · opioid-sparing
Adult
  • Mild / elderly >75 / fragile: 0.1–0.3 mg/kg IV over 1–2 min
  • Moderate–severe / stable: 0.3–0.5 mg/kg IV over 1–2 min
  • Reassess at 5 min; repeat only if needed
Pediatric
  • Young / fragile IV: 0.1–0.3 mg/kg IV over 1–2 min
  • Stable moderate–severe IV: 0.3–0.5 mg/kg IV over 1–2 min
  • IN route: 1–1.5 mg/kg IN (max 1 mL per nostril)
  • Reassess at 5 min; repeat only if needed
NOTEFor pain refractory to opioid analgesia, or as an opioid-sparing adjunct.
NOTEUse for patients on Naltrexone or Buprenorphine.
NOTESub-dissociative doses of ketamine are not advised in patients experiencing psychosis.
NOTEContraindicated for analgesia in pregnancy — use the OB addendum.
⚠ CAUTION Sub-dissociative doses of ketamine for pain control must be given slowly over 1–2 min. If pushed too fast, partial dissociation can occur, which is very disturbing to the patient and can result in agitation and delirium.
Ketorolac (Toradol) NSAID · non-narcotic adjunct · opioid-sparing
Adult
  • 15 mg IV over 30–60 seconds
  • Single dose for transport (do not exceed 5 days total therapy)
Pediatric
  • 0.5 mg/kg IV over 30–60 seconds
  • Max single dose 15 mg
  • Age ≥ 2 yr only
INDICATIONMild-moderate pain (musculoskeletal, renal colic, headache); opioid-sparing in poly-trauma.
CONTRARenal impairment (Cr > 1.5 or AKI), active GI bleed / ulcer, coagulopathy, severe asthma, hypovolemia / hemorrhagic shock.
CONTRAPregnancy > 20 wk (avoid) and > 28 wk (strict — fetal renal dysfunction, premature ductus closure).
Acetaminophen IV (Ofirmev) non-narcotic · non-NSAID · safe in pregnancy
Adult
  • 1000 mg IV over 15 min q 6 hr (≥ 50 kg)
  • < 50 kg: 15 mg/kg IV over 15 min q 6 hr
  • MAX 4 g per 24 hr (all sources combined)
Pediatric
  • ≥ 13 yr or ≥ 50 kg: adult dosing
  • 2–12 yr: 15 mg/kg IV over 15 min q 6 hr
  • MAX single 15 mg/kg · MAX 75 mg/kg / 24 hr (not to exceed 3.75 g)
INDICATIONMild-moderate pain, fever, opioid-sparing adjunct. Safe in pregnancy at all gestational ages. Preferred non-opioid in OB.
CAUTIONHepatic impairment, chronic alcohol use, malnutrition. Account for ALL acetaminophen sources (PO, IV, combo products) toward the 24-hr max.
NOTEInfuse over 15 min — bolus push not recommended. Onset ~5-10 min, peak ~30 min, duration 4-6 hr.

Obstetric Addendum

Pregnant patient — institutional guidance · ketamine contraindicated · NSAID restrictions by gestational age
Non-Labor Pain in Pregnancy
  • Fentanyl 0.5–1 mcg/kg IV; repeat q 5–10 min PRN; max cumulative 200 mcg
  • Hydromorphone 0.2–0.8 mg IV over 2–3 min; repeat q 30–60 min PRN
  • Acetaminophen IV 1000 mg over 15 min q 6 hr — safe in pregnancy at all GA; preferred non-opioid
  • Ketamine — CONTRAINDICATED for analgesia in pregnancy
  • Ketorolac / NSAIDs — avoid > 20 wk, strict > 28 wk (fetal renal, premature ductus closure)
  • Left-lateral tilt > 20 wk; document FHR pre & post each opioid dose
Labor Pain
  • Neuraxial first-line (epidural / spinal) — coordinate with anesthesia/OB
  • Fentanyl 50–100 mcg IV q 30–60 min PRN if neuraxial unavailable
  • Hydromorphone 0.5–1 mg IV q 2–3 hr PRN (alternative)
  • Acetaminophen IV 1000 mg over 15 min q 6 hr as opioid-sparing adjunct
  • NO ketamine for labor analgesia
  • NO NSAIDs (ketorolac) after 20 wk
  • Avoid opioids near planned delivery → neonatal respiratory depression. Have neonatal naloxone 0.1 mg/kg ready.

Reassess at every dose

  • Pain score (NRS adult; FLACC / Wong-Baker peds; verbal in labor)
  • RR & SpO2 — adult < 10, peds age-specific → hold next dose, consider naloxone
  • Sedation level (RASS or Pasero scale)
  • BP & HR — opioid hypotension in the volume-depleted / hemorrhagic patient
  • OB: FHR before AND after each opioid dose; signs of imminent delivery

Document at every administration

  • Medication, dose (units), route, time
  • Patient weight (kg) used for calculation
  • Pre- and post-dose pain score with timestamps
  • RR, SpO2, BP, sedation level pre & post
  • OB: gestational age, FHR pre/post, L-lateral tilt > 20 wk
  • Adverse effects (n/v, pruritus, dissociation, hypotension)
  • Naloxone availability at bedside (esp. peds & OB near delivery)
