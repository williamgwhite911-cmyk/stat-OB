5.1.2 Analgesia

Assess pain frequently during transport to gauge severity and monitor the response to interventions. Pain assessment should include the use of scales appropriate for the age group and patient condition. Pain should be treated to a level as low as reasonably possible in balance with the patient's cardiovascular stability and pain goals.

Fentanyl stock 50 mcg/mL
Adult
  • 0.5–2 mcg/kg IBW IV over 1 min, IM, NAS
  • Repeat every 10 minutes, PRN
Pediatric
  • Refer to adult dosing
Morphine stock 4 mg/mL
Adult
  • 2–10 mg IV push over 1–2 min, IM
  • Repeat every 20 minutes PRN
  • Titrate to pain goal
Pediatric
  • 0.05–0.2 mg/kg IV over 1–2 min, IM
  • Max single dose 10 mg
  • Repeat every 10 minutes PRN
  • Titrate to pain goal
Hydromorphone stock 2 mg/mL or 4 mg/mL · longer-acting
Adult
  • 0.2–1 mg IV over 2 min, IM
  • Repeat q 15 min PRN
  • Max total dose 4 mg
Pediatric
  • 0.01–0.02 mg/kg IV over 2 min, IM
  • Repeat q 15 min PRN
  • Max single dose 1 mg
  • Max total dose 4 mg
Adjunctive Analgesia
Ketamine — Sub-dissociative Dose stock 500 mg / 10 mL (50 mg/mL)
Adult
  • 0.1–0.3 mg/kg IBW IV over 1–2 min, IM, NAS
  • Repeat q 10–20 min PRN
  • Max single dose 30 mg
Pediatric
  • Refer to adult dosing
NOTEFor pain refractory to opioid analgesia.
NOTEKetamine should be used for patients on Naltrexone or Buprenorphine.
NOTESub-dissociative doses of ketamine is not advised in patients experiencing psychosis.
⚠ CAUTION Sub-dissociative doses of ketamine for pain control must be given slowly. If not, partial dissociation can occur, which is very disturbing to the patient and can result in agitation and delirium.
Ketorolac NSAID
Adult
  • 15 mg IV over 30–60 seconds
Pediatric
  • 0.5 mg/kg IV over 30–60 seconds
  • Max dose 15 mg

Obstetric Addendum

Not in PHI 5.1.2 — institutional guidance for the pregnant patient
Non-Labor Pain in Pregnancy
  • Fentanyl 0.5–1 mcg/kg IV; repeat q 10 min PRN; max cumulative 200 mcg
  • Hydromorphone 0.2–0.8 mg IV over 2–3 min; repeat q 30–60 min PRN
  • Ketamine — CONTRAINDICATED for analgesia in pregnancy
  • Ketorolac / NSAIDs — avoid > 20 wk, strict > 28 wk (fetal renal, premature ductus closure)
  • Left-lateral tilt > 20 wk; document FHR pre & post each dose
Labor Pain
  • Neuraxial first-line (epidural / spinal) — coordinate with anesthesia/OB
  • Fentanyl 50–100 mcg IV q 30–60 min PRN if neuraxial unavailable
  • Hydromorphone 0.5–1 mg IV q 2–3 hr PRN (alt)
  • NO ketamine for labor analgesia
  • Avoid near planned delivery → neonatal respiratory depression. Have neonatal naloxone 0.1 mg/kg ready.

Reassess at every dose

  • Pain score (NRS adult; FLACC / Wong-Baker peds; verbal in labor)
  • RR & SpO2 — adult < 10, peds age-specific → hold next dose, consider naloxone
  • Sedation level (RASS or Pasero scale)
  • BP & HR — opioid hypotension in the volume-depleted / hemorrhagic patient
  • OB: FHR before AND after each opioid dose; signs of imminent delivery

Document at every administration

  • Medication, dose (units), route, time
  • Patient weight (kg) used for calculation; IBW where PHI specifies
  • Pre- and post-dose pain score with timestamps
  • RR, SpO2, BP, sedation level pre & post
  • OB: gestational age, FHR pre/post, L-lateral tilt > 20 wk
  • Adverse effects (n/v, pruritus, dissociation, hypotension)
  • Naloxone availability at bedside (esp. peds & OB near delivery)
